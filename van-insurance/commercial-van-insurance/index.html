Commercial Van Insurance

Commercial Van Insurance

If your business owns or uses a van — whether that's one vehicle for a sole trader or a small fleet for a growing company — you'll need a policy built for business use rather than a personal car insurance policy. Commercial van insurance is designed specifically for vans used to carry tools, goods, equipment, or employees as part of a trade or business.

This guide covers what commercial van insurance is, who needs it, what it typically includes, and what affects the price.

  • --

What Is Commercial Van Insurance?

Commercial van insurance is a category of commercial vehicle insurance built around the specific risks of using a van for work. It differs from personal van cover mainly in how "use" is defined — a personal policy typically only covers social, domestic, and commuting use, while a commercial policy covers the van being used to carry out business activity, transport goods, or travel between multiple work sites in a day.

Within commercial van insurance, cover is usually structured around a "use class," which describes how the van is actually used:

  • Carriage of own goods — carrying your own business's tools, stock, or equipment
  • Business use — travelling between multiple sites or client locations as part of the job
  • Courier / hire and reward — carrying goods belonging to someone else for payment (see our courier van insurance guide)

Choosing the wrong use class is one of the most common reasons commercial van claims are challenged, so it's worth being precise about how the van is actually used before comparing policies.

Who Needs Commercial Van Insurance?

You're likely to need commercial van insurance if:

  • You're a sole trader or limited company using a van for your trade (electrician, plumber, builder, carpenter, and similar)
  • Your business owns one or more vans used by employees for work
  • You carry tools, stock, or equipment belonging to the business
  • You travel between multiple client sites or job locations during the working day
  • You occasionally or regularly carry goods for payment (which typically needs hire-and-reward cover, not standard business use)

Both sole traders and limited companies can take out commercial van insurance — the structure of the business affects how the policy is set up (e.g. named driver vs. company-wide cover) rather than whether commercial cover is needed at all.

What Does Commercial Van Insurance Typically Cover?

Cover type What it generally includes
Third party Damage or injury you cause to others — the legal minimum
Third party, fire and theft The above, plus your own van if stolen or damaged by fire
Comprehensive The above, plus damage to your own van regardless of fault
Tools and equipment cover Cover for tools kept in the van, often as an add-on (see goods in transit)
Any driver / named driver Whether cover applies to a named individual or any employee driving with permission
Multi-van cover A single policy covering several vans, useful once a business grows beyond one or two vehicles

Businesses with tools or stock frequently left in the van overnight should check whether tools cover is included or needs adding separately — this is a common gap between basic and fuller commercial van policies.

Common Exclusions to Be Aware Of

  • Using the van for hire-and-reward work without declaring it (e.g. taking on paid delivery jobs on a "business use" policy)
  • Driving by someone not named or not covered under an "any driver" policy
  • Carrying tools or goods above a declared value without additional cover
  • Modifications to the van (racking, signage, conversions) not disclosed to the insurer
  • Overnight parking away from the address declared on the policy

What Affects the Cost of Commercial Van Insurance?

  • Van size, age, and value — larger or higher-value vans generally cost more to insure
  • Use class — carriage of own goods is typically cheaper than hire-and-reward or courier use
  • Driver history — points, claims, and years of experience all factor in
  • Number of vans and drivers on the policy
  • Where the van is kept overnight — postcode and off-street vs. on-street parking affect risk
  • Security features — trackers, immobilisers, and dashcams can influence pricing
  • No claims history

For a closer look at what drives van insurance pricing, see our cost guide.

How to Compare Commercial Van Insurance Properly

Before comparing quotes, it helps to be clear on:

1. The exact use class (own goods, business use, or hire and reward) 2. Whether the policy needs to cover one van or several 3. Whether tools, stock, or equipment need separate cover 4. Any driver history, points, or claims that could affect available options 5. Whether the business is a sole trader or limited company, and how that affects policy structure
