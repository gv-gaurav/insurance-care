Taxi Insurance UK

Taxi Insurance

Driving passengers for a fare — whether as a licensed private hire driver, a public hire (hackney carriage) driver, or through an app-based platform — needs a specific type of cover. Standard car insurance, and even standard commercial vehicle insurance, generally won't cover carrying fare-paying passengers without taxi-specific hire and reward cover in place.

This guide explains what taxi insurance covers, the difference between private and public hire, and what shapes the cost.

  • --

What Is Taxi Insurance?

Taxi insurance is a category of commercial vehicle insurance built specifically for vehicles licensed to carry fare-paying passengers. It falls under a passenger-carrying form of hire and reward, and it's typically split into two main licence types:

  • Private hire insurance — for vehicles booked in advance through an operator, app, or dispatch system (minicabs, app-based platforms)
  • Public hire insurance — for licensed hackney carriages that can be hailed directly on the street or from a taxi rank

The licence type affects both the insurance requirements and how the vehicle can legally pick up passengers, so it's worth being clear on which licence applies before comparing cover.

Who Needs Taxi Insurance?

You're likely to need taxi insurance if you:

  • Hold a private hire driver and vehicle licence issued by a local licensing authority
  • Hold a public hire (hackney carriage) licence
  • Drive for an app-based private hire platform
  • Operate a taxi fleet, whether private hire, public hire, or a mix (see our taxi fleet insurance guide)
  • Are newly licensed and setting up cover for the first time

A standard personal or business-use car insurance policy does not cover carrying fare-paying passengers — this activity needs to be specifically insured, regardless of how often you drive for hire.

What Does Taxi Insurance Typically Cover?

Cover type What it generally includes
Third party Damage or injury you cause to others — the legal minimum
Third party, fire and theft The above, plus your own vehicle if stolen or damaged by fire
Comprehensive The above, plus damage to your own vehicle regardless of fault
Public liability Cover if a passenger or third party is injured or their property is damaged during a journey
Passenger liability Specific cover for injury to fare-paying passengers
Loss of licence cover Some policies offer cover for loss of income if your licence is suspended, often as an add-on

Because taxi work involves carrying members of the public, public liability and passenger liability cover are central to most taxi policies — not optional extras in the way they might be for van insurance.

Common Exclusions to Be Aware Of

  • Driving for hire without the correct licence type declared (private hire vs. public hire mismatches)
  • Working for a platform or operator not disclosed to the insurer, where the policy requires this
  • Using a personal or standard business policy for taxi work, assuming fare-paying journeys are covered
  • Gaps in cover when switching between operators or platforms
  • Vehicle modifications required for licensing (signage, meters, partitions) not declared

What Affects the Cost of Taxi Insurance?

  • Licence type — private hire and public hire are priced differently
  • Driver history — points, claims, and years of experience
  • Vehicle type — including whether it's a purpose-built taxi, a standard car, or a specialist vehicle
  • Location and licensing authority — different areas carry different risk profiles
  • Mileage, which tends to be higher than average for taxi work
  • No claims history
  • Vehicle fuel type — hybrid and electric taxis are increasingly common and can affect pricing

For a closer look at what drives taxi insurance pricing, see our cost guide.

How to Compare Taxi Insurance Properly

Before comparing options, it helps to be clear on:

1. Whether your licence is private hire, public hire, or you're newly applying 2. Which platform(s) or operator(s), if any, you work through 3. Whether you need cover for a single vehicle or a small fleet 4. Any driving history or claims that could affect available options 5. Whether loss-of-licence cover matters to you
