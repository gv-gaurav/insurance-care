Private Hire Insurance UK

Private Hire Insurance

Private hire covers vehicles that are booked in advance — through an operator, a dispatch system, or an app — rather than hailed directly on the street. If you hold a private hire driver and vehicle licence, or drive through an app-based platform, you need insurance that specifically covers this type of licensed passenger work, distinct from both standard taxi insurance generally and from public hire cover.

This guide explains what private hire insurance is, who needs it, what it typically covers, and the exclusions private hire drivers most often run into.

  • --

What Is Private Hire Insurance?

Private hire insurance is cover for vehicles licensed to carry fare-paying passengers on a pre-booked basis — meaning the journey has to be arranged in advance through a licensed operator, dispatch system, or app, rather than picked up directly from the street or a taxi rank. This is the key legal and insurance distinction from public hire (hackney carriage) vehicles.

Private hire licensing and insurance typically apply to:

  • Minicabs booked through a local operator
  • Vehicles driving for app-based private hire platforms
  • Chauffeur and executive car services operating under a private hire licence
  • Airport transfer and pre-booked corporate travel services

For a direct side-by-side comparison of how private hire differs from public hire in practice, see private hire vs. public hire insurance.

Who Needs Private Hire Insurance?

You're likely to need private hire insurance if you:

  • Hold a private hire driver licence and a private hire vehicle licence from a local licensing authority
  • Drive for an app-based private hire platform
  • Work through a minicab or dispatch operator
  • Provide chauffeur, executive, or airport transfer services under a private hire licence
  • Are newly licensed and setting up cover for the first time

Licensing requirements are set by local authorities and can vary by area, but the insurance principle is consistent: carrying fare-paying passengers on a pre-booked basis needs private-hire-specific cover, not a standard business-use or public hire policy.

What Does Private Hire Insurance Typically Cover?

Cover type What it generally includes
Third party Damage or injury you cause to others — the legal minimum
Third party, fire and theft The above, plus your own vehicle if stolen or damaged by fire
Comprehensive The above, plus damage to your own vehicle regardless of fault
Public liability Cover if a passenger or third party is injured or their property is damaged during a journey
Passenger liability Specific cover for injury to fare-paying passengers
Loss of licence cover Cover for loss of income if your licence is suspended, often available as an add-on

Some private hire policies are structured around a specific operator or platform, so it's worth checking whether the cover applies broadly or only while working through a named operator.

Common Exclusions to Be Aware Of

  • Driving for an operator or platform not disclosed to the insurer, where the policy requires this
  • Picking up passengers without a pre-booking, which can fall outside a private hire licence and its associated cover
  • Using a public hire or standard business policy for private hire work, assuming it's covered
  • Gaps in cover when switching between operators or platforms
  • Vehicle modifications required for licensing (signage, meters, livery) not declared

What Affects the Cost of Private Hire Insurance?

  • Operator or platform worked through, where relevant
  • Driver history — points, claims, and years of experience
  • Vehicle type and value
  • Location and licensing authority — different areas carry different risk profiles
  • Mileage, which tends to be higher than average for private hire work
  • No claims history
  • Vehicle fuel type — hybrid and electric private hire vehicles are increasingly common

For a closer look at what drives private hire insurance pricing, see our cost guide.

How to Compare Private Hire Insurance Properly

Before comparing options, it helps to be clear on:

1. Which operator(s) or platform(s), if any, you work through 2. Whether you need cover for a single vehicle or a small fleet (see taxi fleet insurance) 3. Any driving history or claims that could affect available options 4. Whether loss-of-licence cover matters to you 5. Your typical working pattern — full-time, part-time, or occasional
