Fleet Insurance UK

Fleet Insurance

Once a business runs more than a couple of vehicles, insuring each one separately becomes harder to manage and often more expensive than it needs to be. Fleet insurance brings multiple vehicles — and often multiple drivers — under a single policy, with one renewal date and one set of terms to manage.

This guide explains what fleet insurance is, who needs it, what it typically covers, and how it compares to insuring vehicles individually.

  • --

What Is Fleet Insurance?

Fleet insurance is a form of commercial vehicle insurance that covers multiple vehicles belonging to the same business under one policy, rather than each vehicle having its own separate policy and renewal date. Fleets can be made up of a single vehicle type (all vans, for example) or a mix of vehicle types — cars, vans, HGVs, or taxis — depending on the business.

Fleet policies are generally split by size and structure:

  • Mini-fleet insurance — typically for businesses with a smaller number of vehicles (often from around 3 upwards)
  • Standard and larger fleet insurance — for businesses running larger numbers of vehicles across one or more vehicle types
  • HGV fleet insurance and taxi fleet insurance — specialist fleet cover for businesses operating specific vehicle categories at scale

The right structure depends on the number of vehicles, how similar they are in use and value, and how many drivers need to be covered.

Who Needs Fleet Insurance?

You're likely to benefit from fleet insurance if:

  • Your business owns or leases three or more vehicles
  • Multiple employees drive company vehicles, whether on a named or any-driver basis
  • You're currently juggling several separate policies with different renewal dates
  • Your business is growing and adding vehicles regularly
  • You operate a mix of vehicle types under one business (e.g. vans and cars, or vans and HGVs)

Fleet insurance isn't only for large corporate operators — many mini-fleet policies are designed specifically for small and growing businesses moving beyond a single vehicle.

What Does Fleet Insurance Typically Cover?

Cover type What it generally includes
Third party Damage or injury you cause to others — the legal minimum
Third party, fire and theft The above, plus your own vehicles if stolen or damaged by fire
Comprehensive The above, plus damage to your own vehicles regardless of fault
Any driver / named driver Whether cover applies to specific named individuals or any employee driving with permission
Goods in transit Often available as an add-on for fleets carrying goods
Replacement vehicle cover Minimising downtime if a fleet vehicle is off the road

One of the main practical benefits of fleet insurance is a single renewal date and consistent terms across all vehicles, which simplifies admin considerably compared to managing several individual policies.

Common Exclusions to Be Aware Of

  • Adding a new vehicle to the fleet without notifying the insurer, which can leave it uninsured until the policy is updated
  • Drivers not listed or not covered under an any-driver policy
  • Vehicles used outside their declared category (e.g. a van on the fleet policy being used for hire-and-reward work without that being declared)
  • Mixed-use fleets where some vehicles need cover types others don't (e.g. goods in transit only being needed for delivery vehicles, not company cars)
  • Gaps when removing a vehicle from the policy if the change isn't processed correctly

What Affects the Cost of Fleet Insurance?

  • Number and type of vehicles — larger or more specialist fleets typically cost more to insure
  • Number of drivers and whether cover is any-driver or named-driver
  • Driver history across the fleet — points and claims history for all drivers
  • Vehicle usage — standard business use, hire and reward, or passenger transport all price differently
  • Fleet claims history
  • Security measures — trackers, telematics, and driver training can all influence pricing

For a closer look at what shapes fleet insurance pricing, see our cost guide.

How to Compare Fleet Insurance Properly

Before comparing options, it helps to be clear on:

1. The exact number and type of vehicles to be covered 2. Whether cover needs to be any-driver or named-driver 3. Whether any vehicles need hire-and-reward, goods-in-transit, or passenger liability cover 4. The business's current claims history across all vehicles 5. Whether a mini-fleet or standard fleet structure fits better — see fleet vs. individual policies
