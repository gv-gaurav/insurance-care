Haulage Insurance UK

Haulage Insurance

Transporting goods belonging to other businesses, for payment, is a different insurance category to carrying your own goods — and getting this distinction wrong is one of the most common gaps in HGV cover. Haulage insurance is built specifically for operators who carry third-party goods as their core business activity.

This guide explains what haulage insurance is, who needs it, what it typically covers, and how it differs from own goods cover.

  • --

What Is Haulage Insurance?

Haulage insurance is a form of HGV insurance for vehicles used to transport goods belonging to other businesses, in exchange for payment. It's the HGV equivalent of hire and reward for vans — the vehicle is carrying someone else's goods commercially, rather than the operator's own stock or equipment.

This is distinct from own goods insurance, which covers HGVs used to transport a business's own goods — for example, a manufacturer moving its own products between sites. See haulage vs. own goods insurance for a direct comparison of the two.

Haulage operations commonly include:

  • Long-distance and national haulage
  • Regional and local delivery haulage
  • Owner-drivers contracted to haulage companies
  • Logistics businesses carrying goods for multiple clients
  • Specialist haulage (e.g. temperature-controlled, hazardous, or oversized loads)

Who Needs Haulage Insurance?

You're likely to need haulage insurance if you:

  • Transport goods belonging to other businesses as your main activity
  • Work as an owner-driver contracted to a haulage company
  • Run a haulage business carrying goods for multiple clients
  • Hold, or are applying for, an operator's licence for haulage work
  • Are setting up a new haulage business and need cover in place before starting

What Does Haulage Insurance Typically Cover?

Cover type What it generally includes
Third party Damage or injury you cause to others — the legal minimum
Third party, fire and theft The above, plus your own vehicle if stolen or damaged by fire
Comprehensive The above, plus damage to your own vehicle regardless of fault
Goods in transit Cover for the value of goods being hauled, central to most haulage policies (see our goods in transit guide)
Public liability Cover if your operations cause injury or property damage to a third party
Employers' liability Often required if you employ drivers

Because haulage involves carrying goods that belong to a client rather than the operator, goods in transit cover tends to be a central part of the policy rather than an optional add-on.

Common Exclusions to Be Aware Of

  • Operating as haulage on a policy structured for own goods use, without this being correctly declared
  • Carrying goods above a declared value without adequate goods-in-transit cover
  • Specialist loads (hazardous, oversized, temperature-controlled) not disclosed, where standard cover may not apply
  • Driving without the correct licence category or CPC qualification recorded
  • Operating without a valid operator's licence where one is required

What Affects the Cost of Haulage Insurance?

  • Type of goods hauled and their typical value
  • Distance and routes — long-distance national haulage vs. local delivery
  • Vehicle weight and type
  • Driver history and experience, including any penalty points
  • Fleet size, if operating more than one vehicle
  • No claims history
  • Operator licence status

For a closer look at what shapes haulage insurance pricing, see our cost guide.

How to Compare Haulage Insurance Properly

Before comparing options, it helps to be clear on:

1. Whether the work is genuinely haulage (third-party goods) or own goods 2. The typical value and type of goods carried 3. Typical routes and distances — local, regional, or long-distance 4. Whether you're insuring a single vehicle or a small fleet 5. Any driving history, licence categories, or claims that could affect available options
