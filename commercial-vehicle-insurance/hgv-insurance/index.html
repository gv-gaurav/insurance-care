HGV Insurance UK

HGV Insurance

Heavy goods vehicles carry higher stakes than standard commercial vans — bigger vehicles, heavier loads, and often higher-value cargo — and the insurance reflects that. Whether you're an owner-driver running a single HGV or a business operating a small fleet of trucks, HGV insurance needs to be matched carefully to how the vehicle is actually used.

This guide explains what HGV insurance is, who needs it, what it typically covers, and what affects the price.

  • --

What Is HGV Insurance?

HGV insurance is a category of commercial vehicle insurance built for heavy goods vehicles — generally those over 3.5 tonnes, including rigid trucks and articulated lorries. It sits alongside two closely related but distinct concepts:

  • Haulage insurance — covering vehicles used to transport goods belonging to other businesses, for payment
  • Own goods insurance — covering vehicles used to transport a business's own goods, rather than goods belonging to third parties

The distinction between haulage and own-goods use matters because it changes the type of cover required — much like the hire-and-reward distinction for vans — so it's worth being clear on which applies before comparing options.

Who Needs HGV Insurance?

You're likely to need HGV insurance if you:

  • Own or operate a heavy goods vehicle over 3.5 tonnes
  • Work as an owner-driver, whether independently or under contract to a haulage business
  • Run a small fleet of HGVs (see our HGV fleet insurance guide)
  • Transport goods on behalf of other businesses (haulage)
  • Transport your own business's goods using an HGV (own goods)
  • Hold, or are applying for, an operator's licence

Both self-employed owner-drivers and larger logistics businesses fall under HGV insurance — the structure of the policy depends on fleet size, use type, and operator licensing rather than business size alone.

What Does HGV Insurance Typically Cover?

Cover type What it generally includes
Third party Damage or injury you cause to others — the legal minimum
Third party, fire and theft The above, plus your own vehicle if stolen or damaged by fire
Comprehensive The above, plus damage to your own vehicle regardless of fault
Goods in transit Cover for the value of goods being carried, particularly important for haulage work
Public liability Cover if your operations cause injury or property damage to a third party
Employers' liability Often required if you employ HGV drivers
Any driver / named driver Whether cover applies to specific named individuals or any qualified employee

Common Exclusions to Be Aware Of

  • Operating as haulage on a policy only covering own goods, or vice versa, without this being correctly declared
  • Carrying goods above a declared value without adequate goods-in-transit cover
  • Driving without the correct licence category or CPC qualification recorded
  • Operating without a valid operator's licence where one is required
  • Vehicle modifications or trailer combinations not declared to the insurer

What Affects the Cost of HGV Insurance?

  • Vehicle weight and type — a 7.5 tonne vehicle is priced differently to an 18 or 26 tonne truck or articulated lorry
  • Haulage vs. own-goods use
  • Driver history and experience, including any penalty points
  • Mileage and typical routes, including long-distance haulage vs. local delivery
  • Goods carried and their typical value
  • Fleet size, if operating more than one HGV
  • No claims history

For a closer look at what shapes HGV insurance pricing, see our cost guide.

How to Compare HGV Insurance Properly

Before comparing options, it helps to be clear on:

1. Whether the vehicle is used for haulage, own goods, or both 2. The vehicle's weight category (e.g. 7.5 tonne, 18 tonne, 26 tonne, articulated) 3. Whether you're insuring a single HGV or a small fleet 4. Any driving history, licence categories, or claims that could affect available options 5. Whether an operator's licence is already in place or still being applied for
