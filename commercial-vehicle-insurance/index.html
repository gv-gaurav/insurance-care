Commercial Vehicle Insurance

Commercial Vehicle Insurance

If you use a vehicle for work — whether that's a single van, a fleet of trucks, a taxi, or a fleet of HGVs — a standard personal car insurance policy won't cover you. Commercial vehicle insurance is built specifically for vehicles used in the course of business, and getting the right type matters both for legal compliance and for making sure a claim doesn't get rejected.

This guide explains what commercial vehicle insurance is, who needs it, what it typically covers, and what shapes the price — so you know what to look for before you compare options.

  • --

What Is Commercial Vehicle Insurance?

Commercial vehicle insurance is a category of motor insurance designed for vehicles used for business purposes rather than purely personal, social, or commuting use. It sits alongside — and is distinct from — several more specific policy types depending on exactly how the vehicle is used:

  • Van insurance — for tradespeople, delivery businesses, and companies running vans for day-to-day operations
  • Fleet insurance — for businesses insuring multiple vehicles under one policy
  • Taxi insurance — for licensed private hire and public hire vehicles
  • HGV insurance — for heavy goods vehicles used in haulage and logistics

The common thread across all of these is that the vehicle is being used to earn income, carry goods for a business, or transport fee-paying passengers — activities a standard personal motor policy typically excludes.

Who Needs Commercial Vehicle Insurance?

You're likely to need a commercial policy — rather than a personal one — if any of the following apply:

  • You drive a van, car, or truck as part of your trade (electrician, plumber, builder, courier, delivery driver)
  • Your business owns or leases vehicles used by employees
  • You carry goods, tools, or equipment belonging to a business
  • You transport fee-paying passengers (taxi, private hire, minicab)
  • You operate goods vehicles over 3.5 tonnes
  • Your vehicle use goes beyond commuting to a single fixed workplace

Sole traders, limited companies, and larger fleet operators all fall under this umbrella — the right policy type depends on the number of vehicles, how they're used, and who's driving them, not on the size of the business alone.

What Does Commercial Vehicle Insurance Typically Cover?

Cover varies by policy and provider, but most commercial vehicle policies are built around these core elements:

Cover type What it generally includes
Third party Damage or injury you cause to others — the legal minimum
Third party, fire and theft The above, plus your own vehicle if it's stolen or damaged by fire
Comprehensive The above, plus damage to your own vehicle regardless of fault
Goods in transit Cover for goods or equipment carried in the vehicle (often an add-on)
Public liability Cover if your business activity causes injury or property damage to a third party (often bundled or added separately)
Breakdown cover Roadside assistance, sometimes included, often optional

Specialist activities — such as courier work paid per delivery, or carrying passengers for hire — usually require specific extensions like hire and reward cover, which a standard business-use policy does not automatically include.

Common Exclusions to Be Aware Of

Every policy differs, but these are exclusions that commonly catch commercial vehicle owners out:

  • Using the vehicle for hire and reward without the right cover (e.g. paid deliveries or passenger transport on a policy that only covers "business use")
  • Driving outside the terms of your named-driver or any-driver policy
  • Carrying goods or passengers not disclosed at the point of taking out the policy
  • Modifications to the vehicle that weren't declared
  • Using the vehicle for an activity outside its declared trade or use class

Because exclusions like these are common causes of rejected claims, it's worth checking the specifics of any policy against exactly how the vehicle is used — not just the vehicle type.

What Affects the Cost of Commercial Vehicle Insurance?

Commercial vehicle insurance pricing is shaped by a combination of factors, including:

  • Vehicle type and value — larger, more expensive, or specialist vehicles typically cost more to insure
  • Use category — courier and hire-and-reward use tends to carry a higher premium than standard business use
  • Driver history — points, claims history, and years of experience all factor in
  • Number of vehicles and drivers — fleet and mini-fleet policies price differently to single-vehicle cover
  • Where the vehicle is kept overnight — postcode and parking arrangements affect risk
  • Security features — trackers, immobilisers, and dashcams can influence pricing
  • No claims history — a clean claims record generally reduces cost over time

For a closer look at what drives pricing up or down, see our cost guide.

How to Compare Commercial Vehicle Insurance Properly

Because "commercial vehicle insurance" covers such a wide range of use cases, the most useful comparison isn't just about price — it's about matching the policy type to how the vehicle is actually used. Before comparing options, it helps to be clear on:

1. Exactly how the vehicle is used (own goods, hire and reward, passenger transport, tools/equipment only) 2. How many vehicles and drivers need to be covered 3. Whether goods in transit or public liability cover is needed alongside motor cover 4. Any driving history or conviction details that could affect available options
